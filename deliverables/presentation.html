Motivation

Surface inspection on steel rolling mills is one of the longest-standing applications of computer vision in heavy industry. The economic case is concrete: a single missed surface defect on a hot-rolled coil can cascade into customer rejects, downstream scrap, and warranty exposure that is several orders of magnitude larger than the cost of the inspection step itself.

DACH industrial Mittelstand context. Mills like Salzgitter, ThyssenKrupp, voestalpine, and ArcelorMittal Eisenhuttenstadt run line-scan inspection systems that increasingly rely on deep encoder-decoder networks. The Severstal Kaggle benchmark (2019) is the standard public reference point for any vendor selling segmentation-based QC to a DACH steel customer. Any new system is implicitly compared against the 0.91 mean-Dice leaderboard ensemble.

Why segmentation, not detection

  • Per-pixel masks let downstream layers attribute defects to specific rollers, lubricant batches, or thermal-cycle events.
  • Defect classes co-occur on the same image, so the four output channels are independent sigmoids, not a softmax.
  • Defect geometry is highly anisotropic (long thin scratches), which favours encoder-decoder architectures with sharp upsampling boundaries.

Class imbalance summary

  • Class 3 (scratches): ~50% of all defect pixels, present on ~5,000 images.
  • Class 4: ~25% of defect pixels, ~750 images.
  • Class 1: ~17% of defect pixels, ~900 images.
  • Class 2: ~8% of defect pixels, ~250 images (the rare class).
  • Background dominates: ~98-99% of all pixels.

Methods

Pipeline

  • Splits. Single 70/15/15 random split at the image level, seed 42. Multilabel-stratified five-fold cross-validation flagged as Phase 2 follow-up.
  • Preprocessing. ImageNet normalisation; horizontal and vertical flip augmentation; brightness/contrast jitter (p=0.3); Gaussian noise on the advanced model only (p=0.2).
  • Loss. Dice + BCE for the baseline; focal Tversky for the advanced model (alpha=0.7 favours false-negative penalty for QC cost asymmetry; gamma=0.75 focal exponent).
  • Optimiser. AdamW with weight decay 1e-5 (baseline), 1e-4 (advanced).
  • Schedule. CosineAnnealingLR (baseline); CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts T0=5, T_mult=2 (advanced).
  • Mixed precision. Disabled for the baseline; enabled for the advanced model under torch.cuda.amp.

Expected failure modes

  • Under-prediction on class 2 due to imbalance.
  • Ragged boundaries on small isolated defects.
  • False positives along the lateral sheet edges (camera vignetting mistaken for faint scaling).

Why focal Tversky

  • alpha > beta: false negatives weighted more than false positives. Right choice for QC where missing a defect is costlier than flagging a false alarm.
  • gamma < 1: down-weights easy examples, concentrates gradient on rare classes.
  • Cosine warm restarts (T0=5, T_mult=2) give a small but reproducible benefit over flat cosine schedules.

Discussion

What the benchmark says

Single SegFormer mit-b3 with focal Tversky loss is expected to outperform a single U-Net (ResNet-34) with Dice + BCE loss on Severstal by the margins reported in the literature. The lift is concentrated on the rare classes; class 3 saturates near the dataset noise floor.

What the benchmark does NOT say

  1. Latency. SegFormer mit-b3 has ~10x the parameters of the baseline. May not fit a 50-100 ms mill QC trigger budget. mit-b0 or EfficientNet-B0 + DeepLabV3+ are the natural production substitutes.
  2. Calibration. Focal Tversky optimises a thresholded metric, not probability calibration. A deployment that uses scores for triage needs Platt scaling or isotonic regression on a held-out fold.
  3. Cross-mill transfer. A model trained on Severstal images is not guaranteed to perform on a different mill's image stream. Typical fine-tuning sample needed: ~200 to 500 labelled target-domain images.
  4. Cost asymmetry. Focal Tversky is biased toward false positives by construction. Whether that bias matches the customer's cost matrix is empirical.

Phase 2 follow-ups

  • Five-fold cross-validation with iterative multi-label stratification.
  • Test-time augmentation (horizontal/vertical flip mean-of-probabilities).
  • Per-class threshold tuning on validation fold.
  • Defect-Mask2Former as a third architecture to bracket the SegFormer result.
  • Knowledge distillation from mit-b3 teacher to mit-b0 student for production latency.
  • Probability calibration (Platt or isotonic).
  • Instance-level evaluation: connected-component pairing with ground truth, per-instance precision/recall.

Conclusion

Phase 1 deliverable. Code-only scaffold for a credible-scale Severstal benchmark: U-Net baseline, SegFormer advanced model, DeepLabV3+ fallback, EDA notebook, 33 verified references, IMRaD manuscript with explicit placeholders, and this presentation.

Severstal is a rare case where the customer already has a published-leaderboard reference point (0.91 mean Dice ensemble). Any vendor selling segmentation-based QC into this domain is implicitly compared against that number. The engineering case has to cover not just model accuracy but integration (PLC handoff, sliding-window inference), feedback loop (operator overrides, canary deployments), and cross-domain robustness (different mill, different camera, different lubricant).

This Phase 1 scaffold is the starting point. Phase 2 will fill in the cross-validated, calibrated, latency-budgeted variant.
